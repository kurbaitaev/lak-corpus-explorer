Research update · what changed

What changed in this research round

The Explorer now has a public, structured PCMLBE layer with 87,266 tokens, 20,606 source analyses, and 1,234 source lemmas. People can search exact wordforms, lemmas, and grammatical tags while every result remains linked to its source.

How to read these numbers. “Audited” is what the source audit recorded. “Staged” is what actually passed machine verification and was imported into the private layer. They are shown separately, and a number is only ever a count — no passage, filename or extracted line is published here.
Live now

The corpus can now search inside Lak words

A search is no longer limited to a flat text match. The structured PCMLBE layer connects attested wordforms to source lemmas and grammatical analyses. This makes it possible to find related forms and grammatical constructions without presenting machine guesses as facts.

What’s new: the Lak Materials collection, described in the open

We thank Professor Victor Friedman for sharing his Lak research collection with the project. The files themselves stay private while their rights are unresolved; what is now public is the analysis and derived research metadata — what every source is, what language and script it uses, how well its text could be extracted, and how it can strengthen the corpus in the future.

All 320 audited items are accounted for: 293 substantive sources catalogued one by one, and 27 system metadata receipts. No scans, documents, audio, paths or unreviewed passages are published or downloadable. One already-public source has new clarity: the PCMLBE corpus is now confirmed under a CC BY-SA 4.0 license, credited to Erwin Komen and Radboud University.

A hidden parallel text is now usable

The Zhirkov 1955 record inside PCMLBE contains 25 aligned Lak–English segments; 21 also preserve Lak in Cyrillic. The earlier website showed only the Latin transcription. All three available fields are now searchable and remain labelled as source-import evidence until expert review.

Professor Victor Friedman confirmed permission to use the research materials supplied for this project. Source-level provenance and quality review remain attached to every derived result.

How parallel sources are found — and what happens next

Some of these materials appear to exist in more than one version: the same work in Lak and in Russian, in Cyrillic and in Latin script, or as a recording with a transcription. A pair of versions is a lead, not a translation: two files sitting next to each other is not proof that their sentences correspond.

    Strongest alignment opportunities

    Permission for the supplied materials is confirmed. These are the source families most likely to yield aligned Lak–Russian material after human pairing and expert review. Each card shows metadata only until those quality steps are complete.

    Before any of this could become public

      Until every step is complete for a given source, its material stays private: not searchable, not published, not exported, and not used for training. The public corpus grows only through material that is already clear to publish.
